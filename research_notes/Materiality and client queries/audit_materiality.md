# Audit materiality (ISA (UK) 320 / 450) and whether non-audit accountants borrow it

Primary texts used: FRC ISA (UK) 320 and ISA (UK) 450, both "Revised June 2016 (Updated September 2025)", published 25 Sept 2025, effective for periods commencing on or after 17 June 2016. Full text was extracted from the FRC PDFs; quotations below are from those PDFs.
- ISA (UK) 320 PDF: https://media.frc.org.uk/documents/ISA_UK_320_Revised_June_2016_Updated_September_2025.pdf (landing page: https://www.frc.org.uk/library/standards-codes-policy/audit-assurance-and-ethics/auditing-standards/isa-uk-320/)
- ISA (UK) 450 PDF: https://media.frc.org.uk/documents/ISA_UK_450_Revised_June_2016_Updated_September_2025.pdf

## 1. What ISA (UK) 320 and ISA (UK) 450 require

### Takeaway
ISA (UK) 320 requires four documented amounts: overall materiality, specific (lower) materiality for sensitive classes or disclosures, performance materiality, and any revisions. It gives only one worked percentage pair: 5% of PBT for a manufacturer and 1% of revenue or expenses for a not-for-profit. ISA (UK) 450 requires the auditor to accumulate every misstatement above a "clearly trivial" amount. It then evaluates them individually and in aggregate, by size and by nature, and documents the clearly trivial threshold. Neither standard sets a percentage for performance materiality or clearly trivial; those percentages come from firm methodologies (section 2).

### Cited Findings
**ISA (UK) 320**
- Para 9(a): performance materiality is "the amount or amounts set by the auditor at less than materiality for the financial statements as a whole to reduce aggregation risk to an appropriately low level". It can also be set below specific materiality levels. — [ISA (UK) 320](https://media.frc.org.uk/documents/ISA_UK_320_Revised_June_2016_Updated_September_2025.pdf)
- Para 9(b), added in the 2025 text: "Aggregation risk – The probability that the aggregate of uncorrected and undetected misstatements exceeds materiality for the financial statement as a whole." — [ISA (UK) 320](https://media.frc.org.uk/documents/ISA_UK_320_Revised_June_2016_Updated_September_2025.pdf)
- Para 10: the auditor "shall determine materiality for the financial statements as a whole". Where misstatements of lesser amounts in particular classes of transactions, account balances or disclosures could reasonably be expected to influence users' decisions, the auditor "shall also determine the materiality level or levels" for them. This is specific materiality. — [ISA (UK) 320](https://media.frc.org.uk/documents/ISA_UK_320_Revised_June_2016_Updated_September_2025.pdf)
- Para 11: the auditor "shall determine performance materiality" for risk assessment and for the nature, timing and extent of procedures. — same source
- Paras 12–13: materiality must be revised when new information would have led to a different amount initially. If it is lowered, the auditor reconsiders performance materiality and the procedures. — same source
- Para 14: the audit file must document overall materiality, specific materiality, performance materiality and any revisions, plus "the factors considered in their determination". — same source
- Para 6: planning materiality "does not necessarily establish an amount below which uncorrected misstatements, individually or in the aggregate, will always be evaluated as immaterial". It adds: "The circumstances related to some misstatements may cause the auditor to evaluate them as material even if they are below materiality." — same source
- Para 4: users are assumed to have reasonable business and accounting knowledge, to understand that statements are prepared "to levels of materiality", to recognise estimation uncertainty, and to make reasonable decisions. — same source
- A4: "A percentage is often applied to a chosen benchmark as a starting point". The factors for choosing the benchmark are:
  - the elements of the financial statements
  - which items users focus on (profit, revenue, net assets)
  - the nature of the entity, its life-cycle stage and industry
  - its ownership and financing (a debt-financed entity points to assets)
  - the volatility of the benchmark
  — same source
- A5: the example benchmarks are PBT, total revenue, gross profit, total expenses, total equity and net asset value. "Profit before tax from continuing operations is often used for profit-oriented entities." When PBT is volatile, "other benchmarks may be more appropriate, such as gross profit or total revenues." — same source
- A6: the benchmark data can include prior periods, period-to-date figures and budgets, adjusted for significant changes. An exceptional rise or fall in profit may justify "a normalized profit before tax from continuing operations figure based on past results." — same source
- A7: for a period longer or shorter than 12 months, materiality relates to that period's statements, so it is not annualised. — same source
- A8, the only percentages in the standard: "the auditor may consider five percent of profit before tax from continuing operations to be appropriate for a profit-oriented entity in a manufacturing industry, while the auditor may consider one percent of total revenue or total expenses to be appropriate for a not-for-profit entity. Higher or lower percentages, however, may be deemed appropriate". — same source
- A9, small entities: "When an entity's profit before tax from continuing operations is consistently nominal, as might be the case for an owner-managed business where the owner takes much of the profit before tax in the form of remuneration, a benchmark such as profit before remuneration and tax may be more relevant." — same source
- A11, triggers for specific materiality:
  - law, regulation or the framework shaping user expectations, "for example, related party transactions, and the remuneration of management and those charged with governance"
  - key industry disclosures
  - separately disclosed areas of focus, such as segments
  — same source
- A12: the auditor may ask those charged with governance and management for their views on these sensitive areas. — same source
- A13: performance materiality "is not a simple mechanical calculation and involves the exercise of professional judgment". It depends on the auditor's understanding of the entity and on "the nature and extent of misstatements identified in previous audits". — same source

**ISA (UK) 450**
- Para 4(a): a misstatement is a difference in "amount, classification, presentation, or disclosure" from what the framework requires, and can arise from error or fraud. — [ISA (UK) 450](https://media.frc.org.uk/documents/ISA_UK_450_Revised_June_2016_Updated_September_2025.pdf)
- Para 5: "The auditor shall accumulate misstatements identified during the audit, other than those that are clearly trivial." — same source
- A2: "'Clearly trivial' is not another expression for 'not material.'" Such items are "of a wholly different (smaller) order of magnitude, or of a wholly different nature" and are "clearly inconsequential, whether taken individually or in aggregate and whether judged by any criteria of size, nature or circumstances. When there is any uncertainty about whether one or more items are clearly trivial, the misstatement is considered not to be clearly trivial." — same source
- A3: the auditor "may designate an amount" below which misstatements need not be accumulated, because their accumulation "clearly would not have a material effect". An item below the amount is still accumulated if it is not trivial "when judged on criteria of nature or circumstances". — same source
- A6: misstatements can be classed as factual (no doubt), judgmental (unreasonable management judgments) or projected (extrapolated from samples under ISA 530). — same source
- A7: a misstatement "may not be an isolated occurrence", for example where it arises from a control breakdown or a widely applied assumption. — same source
- A8: if accumulated misstatements approach materiality, the risk that undetected misstatements push the total over materiality may be unacceptably high. — same source
- Para 11: the auditor determines whether uncorrected misstatements are material "individually or in aggregate". It considers size and nature against particular classes or disclosures and the statements as a whole, plus prior-period uncorrected misstatements. — same source
- A19: an individually material misstatement is unlikely to be offset by others. Offsetting within the same account may be appropriate, but only after considering the risk of further undetected misstatements. — same source
- Footnote 13: "The identification of a number of immaterial misstatements within the same account balance or class of transactions may require the auditor to reassess the risk of material misstatement for that account balance or class". — same source
- A21: circumstances that can make a below-materiality misstatement material:
  - it affects regulatory compliance
  - it affects debt covenants or other contracts
  - it is a wrong accounting policy with future material effect
  - it masks a change in earnings or trends
  - it affects key ratios
  - it affects segment information
  - "Has the effect of increasing management compensation"
  - it affects previous communications to users, such as forecasts
  - it "Relates to items involving particular parties (for example, whether external parties to the transaction are related to members of the entity's management)"
  - it is an important omission
  - it affects other information in the annual report

  The list is not exhaustive, and the presence of a circumstance "does not necessarily lead to a conclusion that the misstatement is material". — same source
- A22: possible fraud is considered "even if the size of the misstatement is not material". — same source
- A23: the cumulative effect of immaterial prior-period uncorrected misstatements may be material now. — same source
- A27: "Where there is a large number of individual immaterial uncorrected misstatements, the auditor may communicate the number and overall monetary effect" instead of the details of each one. — same source
- Paras 12–15: uncorrected misstatements are communicated to those charged with governance, and the auditor requests their correction. A written representation that they are immaterial is obtained, with a summary attached. The file documents the clearly trivial amount, all accumulated misstatements and whether they were corrected, and the auditor's conclusion. — same source
- A26: where the managers are also those charged with governance, as in an owner-managed company, the communication need not be repeated. — same source

### Inferences
- Para 14 of ISA 320 and para 15 of ISA 450 together define the data model an automated tool would need to hold: overall materiality, specific materialities, performance materiality, the clearly trivial amount, the factors and rationale, and a misstatement log with corrected/uncorrected status.
- The "any uncertainty means not trivial" rule (A2) maps directly to a conservative default in software. If a small item has any nature flag (related party, director, tax-sensitive, possible fraud), it should not be auto-cleared as trivial.

### Gaps
- I did not compare the 2025 update with the 2022 text line by line. The aggregation risk definition in para 9(b) appears in the 2025 text, but I did not confirm which update introduced it.

## 2. Common benchmarks and percentages used in practice

### Takeaway
The FRC's 2017 thematic review gives the authoritative UK spread. Across the 8 largest firms, the ranges were:
- PBT: 3–10%, with 5% most common
- revenue or expenses: 0.25–5%, mostly 0.5–2%
- net assets or equity: 0.5–10%
- performance materiality: 40–90% of overall materiality, mostly 50–75%
- clearly trivial: 0–5% of overall materiality

US big-firm manuals (Eilifsen & Messier 2015) converge on 5% of pre-tax income and 50–75% for tolerable misstatement.

### Cited Findings
- FRC Audit Quality Thematic Review: Materiality (Dec 2017) covered 8 firms and 32 PIE audit files. Firm guidance ranges by benchmark:

  | Benchmark | PIEs | Non-PIEs |
  |---|---|---|
  | PBT | 3–10% across firms (e.g. "up to 5%", 3–5%, 5–10%) | up to 0–10% |
  | Revenue/expenses | 0.25–5% (most firms 0.5–2%) | 0.25–5% (most 0.5–3%) |
  | Net assets/equity | 0–5% | up to 10% |

  — [FRC 2017 thematic review (HTML conversion)](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/3355/html/) (the FRC labels this an unverified AI conversion of the PDF)
- Same review: "The most common percentage applied to profit before tax is 5%". Investors typically expect materiality of up to 5% of PBT, and the FRC recommends that firms justify anything above 5% on a profit benchmark. — [FRC 2017](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/3355/html/)
- Same review, performance materiality as a percentage of overall materiality, by firm:
  - Firm A: maximum 70%
  - Firm B: 40–75%
  - Firm C: 50% or 75%
  - Firm D: 50–75%
  - Firm E: 50–80%
  - Firm F: 90% (not used for PIEs), 75% or 50%
  - Firm G: 50–75%
  - Firm H: 40–75%

  Three firms reduce performance materiality for first-year audits, which the FRC calls good practice. — [FRC 2017](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/3355/html/)
- Same review: all 8 firms set clearly trivial at 0–5% of overall materiality. — [FRC 2017](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/3355/html/)
- Same review: in a worked example, the firms' ranges allowed materiality from about £1m to £25m on the same entity. Higher-benchmark firms could set materiality about 100% higher than prudent firms. Three firms picked the top of their ranges without entity-specific justification. — [FRC 2017](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/3355/html/); summary at [IAS Plus](https://www.iasplus.com/en-gb/news/2017/12/frc-audit-quality-thematic-review-materiality)
- FRC 2013 thematic review covered the 6 largest firms and 26 audits:
  - PBT was the benchmark in 15 audits, revenue in 8 of the remaining 11, and EBITDA, net assets and total assets in 1 each.
  - Four firms defaulted to the maximum performance materiality, with firm maximums of 75–90%.
  - Clearly trivial was set at 1% of materiality in 3 audits, 2% in 7 and 5% in 16. Most firms capped it at 5% and one at 10%.

  — [FRC 2013 thematic review (HTML conversion)](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/1113/html/); [FRC news release](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2013/12/frc-issues-report-on-auditors-materiality-judgements/)
- Eilifsen & Messier (2015), *Materiality Guidance of the Major Public Accounting Firms*, Auditing: A Journal of Practice & Theory 34(2):3–26:
  - Six of the eight largest firms' manuals "expect, suggest, or require the use of 5 percent of income before taxes".
  - Seven firms set tolerable (performance) misstatement within 50–75% of overall materiality, and one uses 70–90%.
  - The authors report "a high level of consistency" across firms, covering benchmarks, percentages and the definition of clearly trivial.

  These figures come from search snippets of the paper and its summaries. I could not open the full tables. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2274845); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275890907_Materiality_Guidance_of_the_Major_Public_Accounting_Firms)
- A University of Richmond paper reports firm guidance treating as material amounts within "3 percent to 10 percent for income before income taxes, 0.25 to 2 percent for total assets, and 0.5 to 5 percent for total revenue". This is from a search snippet only. — [UR Scholarship Repository](https://scholarship.richmond.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1037&context=accounting-faculty-publications)
- The CPA Ireland P1 examiner article (D. McWall, "Materiality and its Practicalities") gives common ranges:
  - revenue 0.5–1%
  - total assets 1–2%
  - net assets 2–5%
  - profit after tax 5–10%
  - not-for-profit or public sector: 0.5–1% of expenses
  - "many auditors are using 60% to 75% of materiality" as performance materiality

  It also says the lower percentage goes with higher risk, and that some auditors average three or four benchmarks. — [CPA Ireland](https://www.cpaireland.ie/CPAIreland/media/Education-Training/Study%20Support%20Resources/P1%20Auditing/Relevant%20Articles/materiality-and-its-practicalities.pdf)
- SEC SAB 99 (1999): the SEC staff does not object to 5% as a "rule of thumb" initial step. It "cannot appropriately be used as a substitute for a full analysis of all relevant considerations", and misstatements below 5% "could well be material". — [SEC SAB 99](https://sec.gov/interps/account/sab99.htm) (wording taken from search results; I did not fetch the page)

### Inferences
- A defensible automated default for a small UK business would be:
  - 5% of PBT, or a normalised or pre-remuneration PBT (A6, A9), as the primary benchmark
  - 0.5–2% of revenue or 1–2% of total assets as fallbacks
  - performance materiality at 50–75% of overall materiality, with the lower end for a first year or where errors were found before
  - clearly trivial at 3–5% of overall materiality, never above 5% given that every firm in the FRC review capped it at 5% for these figures

  The clearly trivial range of "3–5%" quoted in the brief matches practice. The FRC found 1–5% actually used and 0–5% in firm guidance.

### Gaps
- I could not obtain the firm-by-firm tables of Eilifsen & Messier (2015), so the exact US clearly trivial percentage is unverified. I found no primary source for the commonly quoted "clearly trivial = 3–5%" beyond the FRC's 0–5% finding.
- I did not retrieve PCAOB AS 2105 or AICPA AU-C 320 text. They are believed to mirror ISA 320 without percentages, but this is unverified here.
- Big-4 published methodology is proprietary. Only FRC-anonymised ranges were available.

## 3. Benchmarks for loss-making or small owner-managed businesses

### Takeaway
ISA 320 explicitly allows normalised PBT where profit is exceptional (A6), gross profit or revenue where PBT is volatile (A5), and profit before owner remuneration and tax where the owner extracts profit as pay (A9). The FRC's 2026 SME sandbox adds that the users of an owner-managed company may be only the owners and lenders. That can justify higher materiality, except near a covenant breach.

### Cited Findings
- A5: for volatile PBT, use "gross profit or total revenues". A6: use "normalized profit before tax ... based on past results". A9: for owner-managed businesses with nominal PBT, use "profit before remuneration and tax". — [ISA (UK) 320](https://media.frc.org.uk/documents/ISA_UK_320_Revised_June_2016_Updated_September_2025.pdf)
- The CPA Ireland article: "a small owner managed business with marginal profits might want to take net profit before tax and owner remuneration as the best measure". Property businesses suit an asset benchmark, and retailers revenue or profit after tax. — [CPA Ireland](https://www.cpaireland.ie/CPAIreland/media/Education-Training/Study%20Support%20Resources/P1%20Auditing/Relevant%20Articles/materiality-and-its-practicalities.pdf)
- FRC Audit & Assurance Sandbox, "Determining materiality for SMEs" (4 June 2026), a summary of discussion rather than FRC guidance:
  - "In owner-managed businesses, users may be limited to owner-managers and providers of finance". The auditor may ask them what they consider material.
  - Materiality "may be higher for companies with concentrated, private ownership and no external finance", or where the parent is the primary user. It may be lower near a borrowing-covenant breach.
  - The 2017 ranges "have become a standard, from which practitioners may not have confidence to deviate", though they were derived from larger entities.
  - For small audits, using a standard percentage may be more efficient and "should not be discouraged".
  - FRC Practice Note 28 covers SME audit materiality, and ICAEW guidance is expected later in 2026.
  - Participants avoided worked examples because they might become templates.

  — [FRC Sandbox](https://www.frc.org.uk/library/innovation-and-improvement-hub/audit-assurance-sandbox/determining-materiality-for-small-and-medium-sized-entities-smes/)
- FRC 2017: the FRC criticised recurring "exceptional" adjustments to profit benchmarks that lacked clear justification. — [FRC 2017](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/3355/html/)

### Inferences
- An automatic benchmark selector could work like this:
  - Use PBT if it is positive, stable and not nominal.
  - Otherwise, use PBT plus directors' remuneration (salary plus employer NIC and pension where identifiable), per A9.
  - Otherwise, or if loss-making, use revenue (0.5–2%) or total expenses (around 1%). Use gross profit where margins are stable.
  - Use total assets (1–2%) for asset-heavy or property companies.
  - Use a normalised PBT, such as a 3-year average, when the current year is exceptional.
- The tool should not stack repeated "exceptional" add-backs (FRC 2017).
- Record the chosen benchmark and the reason, as para 14 requires the "factors considered".

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative numeric percentage specifically for loss-making SMEs beyond the general revenue and expense ranges.
- I did not retrieve Practice Note 28 or the pending ICAEW SME materiality guidance.

## 4. Qualitative materiality: items material by nature

### Takeaway
The standards name related party transactions, management and director remuneration, regulatory compliance, covenant effects, management compensation effects, items with management-related parties, and possible fraud as circumstances where small amounts can be material. Director expenses and benefits in kind map directly onto "remuneration of management and those charged with governance" (ISA 320 A11) and "related parties" (ISA 450 A21). Tax-sensitive items such as entertainment map onto "compliance with regulatory requirements" (A21). Neither standard names entertainment specifically.

### Cited Findings
- ISA 320 A11 names "related party transactions, and the remuneration of management and those charged with governance" as candidates for lower, specific materiality. — [ISA (UK) 320](https://media.frc.org.uk/documents/ISA_UK_320_Revised_June_2016_Updated_September_2025.pdf)
- ISA 450 A21 names, among other circumstances, regulatory compliance, covenants, "increasing management compensation", and "items involving particular parties (... related to members of the entity's management)". A22 covers fraud "even if the size of the misstatement is not material". A25 (public sector) covers items relating to "compliance with law, regulation". — [ISA (UK) 450](https://media.frc.org.uk/documents/ISA_UK_450_Revised_June_2016_Updated_September_2025.pdf)
- ISA 450 A3: an amount below the trivial threshold is still accumulated if it is not trivial "when judged on criteria of nature or circumstances". — same source
- The CPA Ireland article: below-materiality items such as related party transactions are "by its very nature ... material to the users" and should be adjusted or disclosed. — [CPA Ireland](https://www.cpaireland.ie/CPAIreland/media/Education-Training/Study%20Support%20Resources/P1%20Auditing/Relevant%20Articles/materiality-and-its-practicalities.pdf)
- FRC 2017: "Qualitative factors, especially user needs, should be the overriding consideration". — [FRC 2017](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/3355/html/)
- SAB 99 lists qualitative factors and says the analysis requires both quantitative and qualitative impact. — [SEC SAB 99](https://sec.gov/interps/account/sab99.htm) (via search results)
- Exam-study sources, which are not authoritative, also list errors that turn a profit into a loss and director transactions as material by nature. — [Pastpaper Hero (ACCA study aid)](https://pastpaperhero.com/resources/acca-aa-materiality-and-audit-strategy-overall-and-performance-materiality-isa-320)

### Inferences
- These are my mapping, not explicit in the standards:
  - Director expenses, director loan account postings and benefits in kind fall under director remuneration, related party and close-company loan disclosures. They should have zero or very low thresholds: always flag, never auto-trivialise.
  - Entertainment is tax-sensitive. It is disallowable for corporation tax and its input VAT is generally blocked, so it falls under "regulatory compliance". Its misclassification matters for the tax computation regardless of size, even if it is immaterial to the accounts.
  - Tax returns have no materiality concept equivalent to audit. A small misposting can still produce an incorrect return.
- A practical design is to put "nature flags" (director, related party, tax-sensitive, possible fraud, profit-to-loss swing, covenant) on items that override the numeric threshold.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative UK source that explicitly ties entertainment or benefits in kind to audit qualitative materiality. The mapping above is inference.
- I did not retrieve HMRC guidance stating that tax computations carry no materiality threshold.

## 5. Do bookkeepers or accounts-preparation work, or software, borrow audit materiality?

### Takeaway
The compilation standard (ISRS 4410) imports the conceptual definition of materiality, "could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users", but sets no numeric threshold and no clearly trivial concept. Audit software (CaseWare and others) automates benchmark × % calculations for audits. I found no authoritative evidence that UK bookkeepers or accounts preparers formally adopt ISA 320/450 percentages. The nearest numeric thresholds in bookkeeping are software write-off limits (Sage 200, default maximum 200) and HMRC's VAT error-correction thresholds, and neither is derived from audit materiality.

### Cited Findings
- ISRS 4410 (Revised), Compilation Engagements, effective for reports dated on or after 1 July 2013:
  - Para 34: if the compiled information is materially misstated or misleading, the practitioner proposes amendments. Para 35: if management refuses, the practitioner withdraws.
  - A48: materiality is considered "in the context of the applicable financial reporting framework". Misstatements are material if "individually or in the aggregate" they could influence users' decisions, and judgments are "affected by the size or nature of a misstatement, or a combination of both".
  - A50: the practitioner's view of materiality depends on the needs of users.
  - No percentages are given, and the engagement does not require the practitioner to verify information.

  — [IAASB ISRS 4410 (Revised)](https://www.ifac.org/system/files/publications/files/B012%202012%20IAASB%20Handbook%20ISRS%204410%20(revised).pdf)
- The IFAC SMP guide to compilation engagements suggests using the tenet of "misleading" as the benchmark when considering materiality in a compilation. — [IFAC Guide to Compilation Engagements](https://www.iaasb.org/system/files/publications/files/IFAC-SMPC-Guide-to-Compilation-Engagements-UL.pdf) (via search snippet)
- CaseWare Audit materiality documentation: the user selects a trial-balance group, such as revenue, or a custom basis, and enters a benchmark percentage. Performance materiality is a percentage of planning materiality. — [CaseWare docs](https://documentation.caseware.com/latest/Audit/en_us/Content/User_Planning/Materiality/t_Documenting_materiality.htm); [CaseWare Cloud "define materiality"](https://www.caseware.com/docs/en/cloud/audit-international/planning/define-materiality)
- The FRC 2017 review found that all firms use templates for setting thresholds. — [FRC 2017](https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/3355/html/)
- Free online calculators implement the chain "overall materiality = benchmark × %", with performance materiality as a share of it and clearly trivial "typically around 5%". These are promotional and low-authority. — [Patron Accounting SA 320 calculator](https://www.patronaccounting.com/tools/audit-materiality-calculator-sa-320)
- Sage 200 lets small customer and supplier balances be written off below a user-set maximum, default 200 in base currency. — [Sage 200 help](https://desktophelp.sage.co.uk/sage200/sage200standard/content/Concepts/HKeepWriteOffSmallValues.htm)
- THP advises against simply writing off a suspense balance to make it disappear; understand the cause first. — [THP](https://www.thp.co.uk/what-is-a-suspense-account/)
- HMRC VAT Notice 700/45: net errors up to £10,000 can be corrected on the next return. Errors up to £50,000 can be corrected that way if they do not exceed 1% of box 6 outputs. Larger or deliberate errors must be notified separately. The GOV.UK page and commentators noted a September 2025 update, which I did not verify. — [GOV.UK VAT Notice 700/45](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-correct-vat-errors-and-make-adjustments-or-claims-vat-notice-70045); [MHA](https://www.mha.co.uk/insights/correcting-vat-errors-hmrcs-tougher-approach)

### Inferences
- Borrowing ISA 320/450 numbers for accounts preparation is defensible as an internal review heuristic. ISRS 4410 uses the same definition and allows size or nature judgments. However, no standard prescribes it for non-audit work, and it should be described as a firm policy.
- "Clearly trivial" is the most transferable concept for bookkeeping, for suspense clearing and for deciding which small items to query. ISA 450 A2 (uncertainty means not trivial) and A3 (nature overrides size) supply the guardrails.
- Tax thresholds, such as VAT correction limits, are a separate regime. A bookkeeping materiality setting should not override tax-correctness for tax-sensitive codes.

### Gaps
- I found no survey or authoritative evidence of UK bookkeepers or accounts preparers using audit materiality percentages, or of mainstream bookkeeping software (Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent) offering a materiality setting.
- I could not retrieve the ICAEW technical release on compilation reports (AAF / TECH guidance) to check for materiality wording. The Bright (accounts-production workpapers) help article on materiality returned HTTP 403, so its content is unknown.

## 6. Aggregation: evaluating many small misstatements together

### Takeaway
ISA 450 requires every misstatement above clearly trivial to be accumulated, and uncorrected misstatements to be judged "individually or in aggregate". Performance materiality exists precisely because many sub-threshold errors can add up to a material one. Clusters of small errors in one account are a signal to reassess risk, and communicating them as a count plus total is expressly allowed.

### Cited Findings
- ISA 320 A13: "Planning the audit solely to detect individually material misstatements overlooks the fact that the aggregate of individually immaterial misstatements may cause the financial statements to be materially misstated, and leaves no margin for possible undetected misstatements." — [ISA (UK) 320](https://media.frc.org.uk/documents/ISA_UK_320_Revised_June_2016_Updated_September_2025.pdf)
- ISA 450 para 5 and A3 require accumulation of all misstatements above the designated trivial amount. Para 11 requires evaluation "individually or in aggregate". — [ISA (UK) 450](https://media.frc.org.uk/documents/ISA_UK_450_Revised_June_2016_Updated_September_2025.pdf)
- ISA 450 A5: misstatements by nature "cannot be added together" like amounts, but must still be evaluated collectively. A18: identified errors may be "persistent or pervasive", or "a number of identified misstatements are relevant to the same matter". — same source
- ISA 450 A7 says a misstatement may not be isolated. A8: aggregate accumulated misstatements approaching materiality raise the risk from undetected ones. Footnote 13: several immaterial misstatements in one account may require reassessment of risk. — same source
- ISA 450 A19: offsetting within the same account may be appropriate only after considering further undetected misstatements. A23: immaterial prior-period errors cumulate. — same source
- ISA 450 A27: a large number of small uncorrected misstatements may be communicated as "the number and overall monetary effect". — same source
- The CPA Ireland article: the aggregate of unadjusted errors is compared with performance materiality to decide whether further adjustment is needed. — [CPA Ireland](https://www.cpaireland.ie/CPAIreland/media/Education-Training/Study%20Support%20Resources/P1%20Auditing/Relevant%20Articles/materiality-and-its-practicalities.pdf)

### Inferences
- For batching small items in software:
  - Items below clearly trivial (around 3–5% of overall materiality) can be auto-accepted, unless a nature flag applies.
  - Items above clearly trivial are logged and summed by account and in total.
  - Raise an alert when the running total per account or overall reaches performance materiality (50–75% of overall materiality), not overall materiality itself.
  - Several small errors in the same account should escalate that account (footnote 13).
  - Client queries for many small items can be batched as a count plus total (A27).
- Prior-year unresolved items should carry forward into the aggregate (A23).

### Gaps
- No standard gives a numeric rule for how many trivial items can be ignored in aggregate. The only test is the qualitative one in A2/A3: their accumulation "clearly would not have a material effect".
