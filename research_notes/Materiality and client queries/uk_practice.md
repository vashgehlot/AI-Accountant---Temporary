# UK practice: coding it yourself vs querying the client (non-audit work: bookkeeping, management accounts, year-end accounts preparation/compilation, VAT)

Research date: 17 Nov 2026. A note on sources: AccountingWEB Any Answers threads block automated fetching (Cloudflare challenge). Points from them below come from search-engine summaries of the threads, not from reading them in full. Treat them as practitioner opinion, of moderate confidence. ICAEW TECH 07/16 AAF is behind a members-only wall, so its text could not be read; ISRS 4410 (Revised) was read in full from the IFAC handbook PDF.

Labels used: **[OFFICIAL]** = standard, regulator or professional-body guidance; **[PRACTITIONER]** = forum, firm blog or vendor; **[INFERENCE]** = my synthesis.

## 1. What do ICAEW / ACCA / AAT / ICAS guidance and ISRS 4410 say about materiality in compilation work? Is a number expected?

### Takeaway
No official UK or international compilation guidance sets a numeric materiality threshold. ISRS 4410 (Revised) frames materiality as a judgement made under the applicable reporting framework: would the misstatement "reasonably be expected to influence" users' decisions, by size or nature? The practitioner does not verify information. They read the compiled accounts against their understanding of the business. If they become aware that information is incomplete, inaccurate or misleading, they must raise it with management and request corrections. That duty is the formal basis for client queries. A numeric threshold is a firm's own working tool, not a regulatory expectation.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL] ISRS 4410 (Revised), para 6:** a compilation "does not require the practitioner to verify the accuracy or completeness" of management's information. Para 7: management keeps responsibility for the financial information. — [IAASB Handbook, ISRS 4410 (Revised)](https://www.ifac.org/system/files/publications/files/B012%202012%20IAASB%20Handbook%20ISRS%204410%20(revised).pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL] ISRS 4410 para 29:** compile "using the records, documents, explanations and other information, including significant judgments, provided by management". Para 30: discuss with management any significant judgements the practitioner assisted with. — [same](https://www.ifac.org/system/files/publications/files/B012%202012%20IAASB%20Handbook%20ISRS%204410%20(revised).pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL] ISRS 4410 para 31:** before completion, read the compiled information "in light of the practitioner's understanding of the entity's business". This is the analytical "does it look right" step that generates review points. — [same](https://www.ifac.org/system/files/publications/files/B012%202012%20IAASB%20Handbook%20ISRS%204410%20(revised).pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL] ISRS 4410 para 32 (the duty to query):** if records or explanations are "incomplete, inaccurate or otherwise unsatisfactory", bring this to management's attention and request additional or corrected information. Para 33: if management fails to provide it, withdraw. — [same](https://www.ifac.org/system/files/publications/files/B012%202012%20IAASB%20Handbook%20ISRS%204410%20(revised).pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL] ISRS 4410 para 34–35:** if amendments are needed so the information is not materially misstated or otherwise misleading, propose them to management. If management declines, withdraw. — [same](https://www.ifac.org/system/files/publications/files/B012%202012%20IAASB%20Handbook%20ISRS%204410%20(revised).pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL] ISRS 4410 A48:** materiality is considered "in the context of the applicable financial reporting framework". Misstatements, including omissions, are material if they "individually or in the aggregate" could reasonably be expected to influence users' economic decisions. The judgement is affected by size or nature, and is based on users as a group. — [same](https://www.ifac.org/system/files/publications/files/B012%202012%20IAASB%20Handbook%20ISRS%204410%20(revised).pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL] ISRS 4410 A50:** the practitioner may assume users understand that financial information is "prepared and presented to levels of materiality", and that estimates carry inherent uncertainty. No percentage or numeric benchmark appears anywhere in the standard. — [same](https://www.ifac.org/system/files/publications/files/B012%202012%20IAASB%20Handbook%20ISRS%204410%20(revised).pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL] ICAEW TECH 07/16 AAF** (Chartered Accountants' Reports on the Compilation of Financial Information of Incorporated Entities, 28 March 2016) is the ICAEW guidance for compiling Companies Act 2006 accounts. TECH 08/16 AAF covers unincorporated entities. Their text is members-only, and I could not confirm what they say on materiality. — [ICAEW technical releases list](https://icaew.com/technical/technical-releases/audit-technical-releases); [TECH 07/16 AAF](https://www.icaew.com/-/media/corporate/files/technical/technical-releases/audit/tech-0716-aaf-compilation-reports-incorporated.ashx); [TECH 08/16 AAF](https://icaew.com/-/media/corporate/files/technical/technical-releases/audit/tech-0816-aaf-compilation-reports-unincorporated.ashx)
- **[OFFICIAL] ICAEW Code of Ethics (integrity):** a member must not knowingly be associated with misleading information. On becoming aware of such an association, the member must take steps to be disassociated from it. The 2026 Code came into force on 1 July 2026. — [ICAEW fundamental principles](https://www.icaew.com/technical/trust-and-ethics/ethics/code-of-ethics/the-fundamental-principles); [ICAEW: 2026 Code in force 1 July](https://www.icaew.com/insights/viewpoints-on-the-news/2026/apr-2026/get-ready-for-2026-update-to-icaew-code-of-ethics)
- **[OFFICIAL] HMRC BIM31045:** HMRC's Business Income Manual treats materiality as an accountancy concept and quotes the FRS 102 Section 2 test (omission or misstatement could influence users' decisions, by size or nature). It warns that immaterial departures from GAAP must not be made, or left uncorrected, to achieve a particular presentation. It distinguishes audit materiality from the judgements preparers make in giving a true and fair view. — [HMRC BIM31045](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-income-manual/bim31045)
- **[OFFICIAL] FRS 102:** para 3.16B (FRC amendment) says a disclosure is not required if the information is not material. Para 2.6 bars immaterial departures made to achieve a particular presentation. — [FRC: differences between FRS 102 and IFRS for SMEs](https://www.frc.org.uk/accountants/accounting-and-reporting-policy/uk-accounting-standards/significant-differences-between-frs-102-and-the-if). A disclosure required by law must still be given regardless of materiality (secondary summary, see Q4 findings).
- **[OFFICIAL] FRS 105** takes its materiality description from IAS 1 (1997) and the Framework. The revised FRS 102/105 apply to periods starting on or after 1 January 2026. — [ICAEW FRS 105 hub](https://icaew.com/technical/corporate%20reporting/uk%20gaap/frs%20105%20the%20financial%20reporting%20standard%20applicable%20to%20micro%20entities); [Blick Rothenberg on 2026 changes](https://www.blickrothenberg.com/insights/detail/changes-affecting-small-company-financial-reporting-periods-commencing-on-or-after-1-january-2026/) (secondary, confirm against FRC text)
- **[OFFICIAL] AAT:** I found no AAT guidance on materiality for bookkeepers. AAT material covers licence scope (for example, a bookkeeper preparing statutory accounts beyond their authorisation breaches the regulations), AML, and professional judgement in general terms. — [AAT Comment on licensed member breaches](https://www.aatcomment.org.uk/?p=58563); [AAT Practice Assurance Standards](https://aat.org.uk/files/Practice-Assurance-Standards.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** The official model is "accept the client's records, don't verify, but don't be associated with anything you know or suspect is materially wrong or misleading". In practice, an accountant codes an item themselves when the answer can be reasonably inferred and the risk of being wrong is immaterial. They query when the information is "incomplete, inaccurate or otherwise unsatisfactory" and the answer could matter, by size or by nature (VAT, tax, related party, director's loan).
- **[INFERENCE]** Because materiality turns on both size and nature, a pure £ threshold is not enough. Items that are small but sensitive need querying whatever their size, for example VAT recovery, director/personal spend, or anything affecting a director's loan account (s455) or benefit-in-kind reporting.

### Gaps
- The exact TECH 07/16 AAF and TECH 08/16 AAF wording on materiality, enquiries and representations (members-only paywall).
- ACCA and ICAS equivalent compilation guidance was not located in the time available. ACCA has an audit-exempt companies factsheet ([ACCA tf-audit-exempt-companies Jan 24](https://www.accaglobal.com/content/dam/ACCA_Global/Technical/fact/tf-audit-exempt-companies-jan-24.pdf)), but I did not read it.
- Verbatim FRS 102 para 2.6 and FRS 105 Section 3 text was not retrieved.

## 2. How do firms set materiality for year-end accounts prep and bookkeeping review in practice?

### Takeaway
Most small practices have no formal numeric materiality for unaudited accounts. Practitioners call it "a basic tenet" applied by judgement, relative to the client's size. Where numbers are used, they are borrowed from audit conventions (about 5% of profit before tax, 0.5–1% of revenue, 1–2% of assets) or set as per-client £ levels for specific areas such as prepayments and capitalisation. Firm quality control is risk-based. ICAEW requires work to be reviewed before it is finalised, and a sample of accounts to be cold-reviewed for being "materially correct". It does not prescribe the threshold.

### Cited Findings
- **[PRACTITIONER]** AccountingWEB posters describe materiality as "a basic tenet of accounts preparation", judged against the particular client, for example "material for their size" for a micro-entity. — [AccountingWEB node/205091 and related threads, via search summary](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/205091)
- **[PRACTITIONER / AUDIT-DERIVED]** Common audit rules of thumb that preparers borrow are 5% of PBT, 0.5–1% of revenue, 0.5–1% or 1–2% of assets, or 5–10% of profit. Profit is a poor base when results are volatile or near break-even, and turnover or an average of past profits may be better. A small error can be material by nature, for example one that turns a profit into a loss. — [OpenTuition materiality topics](https://opentuition.com/topic/calculation-of-materiality/); [MAH on materiality](https://www.mah.uk.com/materiality/); [CPA Ireland "Materiality and its practicalities"](https://www.cpaireland.ie/CPAIreland/media/Education-Training/Study%20Support%20Resources/P1%20Auditing/Relevant%20Articles/materiality-and-its-practicalities.pdf). One UK practice site gives a sliding scale (3% of turnover or gross assets up to £500k; 2.5% from £500k to £2m), but I could not verify its basis. — [MAH](https://www.mah.uk.com/materiality/)
- **[OFFICIAL, audit context]** The FRC has a sandbox project on determining materiality for SMEs, confirming even audit materiality for SMEs is judgement-heavy. It is audit-focused, not compilation. — [FRC: determining materiality for SMEs](https://www.frc.org.uk/library/innovation-and-improvement-hub/audit-assurance-sandbox/determining-materiality-for-small-and-medium-sized-entities-smes/)
- **[OFFICIAL] ICAEW Practice Assurance Standard 4 (smaller firms, published 1 May 2018):**
  - All firms except sole practitioners with no staff should ensure work is supervised and reviewed before it is finalised.
  - Firms should "formulate your policy for reviews on a risk basis".
  - Senior staff usually review juniors. A principal's work may be reviewed by another principal, depending on risk.
  - Competent staff's work need not all be hot-reviewed, but a sample should be reviewed cold. Cold reviews of high-risk assignments are essential.
  - Compliance reviews should sample accounts to check they are materially correct and statutorily compliant.
  - Firms should document significant judgements and keep evidence of review, or a file note that a review happened.
  - Sole practitioners should use reciprocal or external reviewers for complex work.
  - — [ICAEW PA Standard 4, smaller firms](https://www.icaew.com/regulation/practice-assurance/practice-assurance-standards-smaller-firms/standard-4-smaller); [PDF version](https://www.icaew.com/-/media/corporate/files/regulations/practice-assurance/guidance-on-pa-standard-4-for-smaller-and-new-firms.ashx)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** One recent AccountingWEB piece on reviewing AI-prepared packs suggests the reviewer should first confirm client, period and scope, then read exceptions before routine items, then trace a sample of figures to source. Untraceable figures mean the pack isn't ready, and each approve/correct/return decision should be recorded with a reason. — [AccountingWEB: How to review AI-prepared accounting work](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/community/industry-insights/how-to-review-ai-prepared-accounting-work) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** The practical decision rule in small UK firms is per-client judgement anchored on the client's size, not a firm-wide %. Reviewers apply audit-style rules of thumb informally (for example, "would it move profit by around 5%?" or "would it change the tax?") plus nature-based overrides. For tax-driven owner-managed businesses, "does it change the tax liability or a VAT return" is often a stronger trigger than accounting materiality. This is consistent with HMRC's stance that immaterial departures must not be used to shape presentation.
- **[INFERENCE]** Partner or manager sign-off on materiality is implicit in the ICAEW risk-based review requirement. There is no evidence of a standard documented "materiality memo" for unaudited accounts in small firms.

### Gaps
- No survey data on how many UK firms set numeric materiality for compilation work.
- No published firm review checklist with explicit compilation thresholds was found, apart from audit ones.

## 3. How do firms run client query lists?

### Takeaway
Query handling is a mix of in-ledger, per-transaction questions (for example Xero's Discuss tab, or Client Hub-style "ask client" tools that flag large items, missing attachments and missing payees) and batched lists: monthly or quarterly for bookkeeping clients, and a single year-end query list or letter for accounts clients. Firms try to cut false flags before sending, keep each query short and specific, and resolve whatever can be inferred themselves. The main client-side complaints are partial answers, late or self-selected information, wrong formats and email overload.

### Cited Findings
- **[PRACTITIONER]** A query list is items the preparer is unsure about or that look unusual, each with evidence and a proposed answer. One example: 40 initial flags reduced to 22 real queries after removing false alarms. — [Sagnik Bhattacharya blog: bookkeeping tax-year-end handoff](https://sagnikbhattacharya.com/blog/bookkeeping-tax-year-end-handoff-ai) (single blog, low authority)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** One UK firm uses Xero's Discuss tab to raise questions in context, with minimal wording such as "What is this?". Xero forum users note the client still has to be told a question is there, so some firms batch unexplained items, sending screenshots 4–5 at a time. One vendor argues queries arise daily and monthly batching lets them pile up while clients forget what transactions were. — [AccountingWEB: Best way to ask clients questions/collate records](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/167077); [Xero bank reconciliation guide](https://www.xero.com/us/accountant-bookkeeper-guides/bank-reconcilation/); [Client Hub blog](https://www.clienthub.app/blog/improve-client-responsiveness-accountants-bookkeepers) (search summaries; vendor sources)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** One AccountingWEB article reports that a firm's portal trial found clients mostly just follow the email link, so the portal must be frictionless. — [AccountingWEB node/232711](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/232711) (search summary)
- **[PRACTITIONER / VENDOR]** Client Hub's Xero workflow is sync data, review exceptions, ask the client, update Xero. Its review flags include large transactions, missing attachments and transactions without payees. — [Client Hub (Xero app)](https://apps.xero.com/us/app/client-hub)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** A UK formations/accounting service divides work explicitly. The client handles routine coding, attaching receipts and weekly bank reconciliation. The client must contact the firm for unusual or one-off transactions, new overseas suppliers, corrections in a period already filed for VAT, and any HMRC correspondence. Its rule is "If in doubt, ask", with no monetary thresholds. — [1st Formations: when to contact us vs handle it yourself](https://help.1stformations.co.uk/accounting-service/when-to-contact-us-vs-when-to-handle-it-yourself)
- **[PRACTITIONER] AAT Comment, 16 Dec 2025** (three practitioners, not a survey) on what clients do that makes queries hard:
  - answering only the first question, or not the one asked
  - deciding themselves what information is needed
  - revealing expenses paid personally late in the job
  - sending PDFs instead of CSVs
  - slow answers combined with expecting accounts within three weeks
  - 14 emails in 30 minutes
  - What practitioners value: linked bank feeds, items supplied on time and in the requested format, and early warning of changes. — [AAT Comment: client behaviour accountants value and find hardest](https://www.aatcomment.org.uk/?p=57835)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Suspense items are expected to be cleared by year end. A suspense credit holding unidentified income understates profit (tax risk). Firms should clear suspense regularly (one source suggests within 30 days), clear it by journal for an audit trail, and fix the rules that cause recurring items to land there. Common causes are unidentified receipts with no payer reference and incomplete supplier invoices. — [THP: What is a suspense account](https://www.thp.co.uk/what-is-a-suspense-account/); [HandiLedger help: clear suspense](https://help-handisoft.theaccessgroup.com/en/articles/12306879-handiledger-clear-the-suspense-account-in-handiledger)
- **[OFFICIAL] ISRS 4410 para 32–33** provides the formal basis for the year-end query: request missing or corrected information, and withdraw if it is not provided. — [ISRS 4410](https://www.ifac.org/system/files/publications/files/B012%202012%20IAASB%20Handbook%20ISRS%204410%20(revised).pdf)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** The typical operating pattern has three layers:
  1. The preparer resolves anything inferable from payee history, the bank narrative or attached documents.
  2. Unresolved items go to a batched list, monthly for bookkeeping clients or once at year end, each with date, amount, narrative and a proposed treatment. This lets the client confirm with one word.
  3. Only material or sensitive items block completion. Immaterial residuals get a judgement call (for example, sundry or general expenses, VAT not reclaimed) recorded on file.
- **[INFERENCE]** Proposing an answer reduces partial answers and turnaround time, which addresses the complaints AAT Comment records.

### Gaps
- No reliable data on a "normal" number of queries per client or per year-end. The 40→22 example is anecdotal.
- No published UK template year-end query letter was retrieved from ICAEW or ACCA.

## 4. Capitalisation policies and prepayment/accrual de minimis practice in small UK businesses

### Takeaway
There is no official £ figure. Capitalisation thresholds in small UK businesses commonly sit between about £100–£250 and £1,000. Some practitioners use far higher figures (one forum contributor never capitalises under £3,000), and charities typically use £500–£1,000. Prepayment and accrual de minimis levels vary with client size, from about £100 for small businesses up to £1,000 (sometimes per month) for larger ones. Many very small clients get few or none. Practitioners set these per client by materiality. The advice is to document them as accounting policies, apply them consistently, and normally use the same de minimis for tax. With AIA and full expensing, the tax cost of expensing a small capital item is usually nil, which reduces the pressure to capitalise.

### Cited Findings
- **[PRACTITIONER] AccountingWEB capitalisation threads** (search summaries; thread dates not visible):
  - Capitalise above £250 with an asset register, and expense small tools to a "small tools" P&L code.
  - "£200 rule of thumb", not applied rigidly, and lower for very small businesses.
  - Others use £1,000. One called £1,000 too low and never capitalises under £3,000.
  - A large business with many purchases might set, for example, £500, while a small business with few purchases should capitalise most items, such as computers.
  - — [AccountingWEB: Lower limit for capitalisation](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/any-answers/lower-limit-for-capitalisation); [Capitalisation of small assets](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/137939); [Depreciation – Capitalisation Threshold](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/216104); [Asset Value less than £500 Capitalisation](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/74531)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** The orthodox approach is for the de minimis limit, "whatever it is", to apply for all purposes, including tax. — [AccountingWEB node/89428](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/89428) (search summary)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Charities: many small and medium charities use £500–£1,000. — [ExpensePlus: fund accounting](https://expenseplus.co.uk/fund-accounting/?p=3647)
- **[OFFICIAL] HMRC:**
  - Low-value items used day to day (for example stationery) are allowable expenses, and capital allowances cannot be claimed on anything already deductible as an expense. — [SA103F Notes 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69c26565cfa346b9d4704b35/SA103F_Notes_2026.pdf)
  - The small pools allowance writes off a main or special rate pool of £1,000 or less. — [HS252 Capital allowances 2024](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/capital-allowances-and-balancing-charges-hs252-self-assessment-helpsheet/hs252-capital-allowances-and-balancing-charges-2024)
  - HMRC acknowledges it can be impractical to apply capital allowances to insubstantial items such as tools or small office equipment (employment context). — [EIM36510](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim36510)
  - Statute does not define "capital item"; the line rests on case law. — [BIM35002](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-income-manual/bim35002)
  - I found no explicit HMRC statement accepting a commercial de minimis write-off of capital items.
- **[OFFICIAL] ICAEW helpsheet:** an accounting policy choice (example: expensing development costs) must be applied consistently. — [ICAEW TAS helpsheet: website development costs under FRS 102](https://www.icaew.com/technical/tas-helpsheets/financial-reporting/can-i-capitalise-website-development-costs-under-frs-102). Firm commentary advises documenting capitalisation-policy decisions. — [MHA on capitalising development costs](https://www.mha.co.uk/insights/commercial-considerations-for-tech-companies-when-capitalising-development-costs-as-intangible-assets)
- **[PRACTITIONER] Prepayments and accruals:**
  - The threshold is where omitting the prepayment would change a user's view of the figures.
  - One poster used £100 per month at a turnover of a few £m, and £1,000 per month at larger scale.
  - Another used £1,000 for their own in-house accounts, lower in a smaller setting. A UK Business Forums poster suggests ignoring prepayments under £100.
  - One commenter says it depends on culture and reporting needs as much as size.
  - Some practitioners say small-business accruals and prepayments "tend to be a whole lot of nothing" and try to avoid them.
  - — [AccountingWEB: Threshold for categorising a prepaid expense](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/any-answers/threshold-for-categorising-a-prepaid-expense-0); [Prepayment tracking 'threshold'?](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/any-answers/prepayment-tracking-threshold); [Prepayments and Accruals](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/any-answers/prepayments-and-accruals); [Prepayment – minimum level?](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/node/164684); [UK Business Forums](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/prepaid-expense-spanning-multiple-years.351130/). All are search summaries; the posts are roughly 2–4 years old.
- **Contrary views on prepayments and accruals:** an older (2006) commentary claims accruals and prepayments are immaterial for almost all small entities. — [Tax Research UK, 2006](https://www.taxresearch.org.uk/Blog/wp-content/uploads/2006/08/blog-post-accounting-for-smaller-businesses.pdf). ACCA study notes counter that they can be material. — [OpenTuition AA notes](https://opentuition.com/acca/aa/notes/aa-19-accruals-and-prepayments/)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Cash basis is now the default for self-employed income tax (from 2024/25), removing accruals for many unincorporated clients. — [AccountingWEB: Cash basis to replace accruals for self-employed](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/tax/hmrc-policy/cash-basis-to-replace-accruals-for-self-employed)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** The common practical stance is:
  - Capitalisation threshold: set per client, typically £250–£1,000 for owner-managed companies, recorded on the permanent file or accounts policies and used for tax too.
  - Prepayments and accruals: posted only for recurring, identifiable items (insurance, rent, software subscriptions, accountancy fee accrual) above a client-specific floor, commonly £100–£500 for micro or small companies.
  - Decision-maker: the manager or partner sets these. The bookkeeper applies them.
- **[INFERENCE]** FRS 105 and FRS 102 1A materiality makes immaterial accruals and prepayments optional. Because HMRC follows GAAP profits (with no immaterial departures used to shape presentation), the tax effect is usually timing-only and small, which is why practitioners tolerate it.

### Gaps
- No survey data on prevalent thresholds. Figures come from forums.
- No official FRC or HMRC numeric guidance exists.

## 5. Expense claims, meals/entertainment, sundry and suspense: what triggers a query?

### Takeaway
The triggers are mostly nature-based, not size-based:
- VAT recovery depends on who attended. Staff entertainment is recoverable, client entertainment is blocked, and mixed events must be apportioned.
- A VAT reclaim needs a valid VAT invoice.
- Directors-only events are not input tax.
- A missing business purpose or attendee list, personal-looking spend, unidentified receipts (possible income), and items with no payee or attachment all prompt queries.

Sundry or general coding of small unknown items is tolerated, below a client-specific level, where no VAT is reclaimed.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL] HMRC VAT Notice 700/65** (last updated 3 June 2026):
  - Input tax on business entertainment of UK contacts, and of non-UK non-customers, is blocked.
  - Staff entertainment (parties, team building, outings) is not blocked.
  - Where staff and guests attend together, only the staff portion is recoverable.
  - Directors and partners count as employees, but entertainment provided only for directors or partners is not input tax. Pensioners, job applicants and non-employee shareholders count as non-employees.
  - Overseas customer entertainment may be recoverable if reasonable in scale and character.
  - — [HMRC VAT Notice 700/65](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/business-entertainment-and-vat-notice-70065)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** On enquiry, HMRC expects itemised receipts plus the names of those entertained and the business purpose. Mixed events are split by headcount between staff and non-staff. — [Ryan: Can you claim VAT on entertainment?](https://ryan.com/insights/claim-vat-on-entertainment); [Sleek: VAT on staff entertainment](https://sleek.com/uk/resources/vat-on-staff-entertainment/)
- **[OFFICIAL]** VAT records must allow recoverable input tax to be calculated each period. — [VAT Notice 706](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/partial-exemption-vat-notice-706). The specific record content is in VAT Notice 700/21, which I did not read.
- **[PRACTITIONER/VENDOR]** Automated review flags used by practice tools include large transactions, missing attachments and missing payees. — [Client Hub](https://apps.xero.com/us/app/client-hub)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Unidentified receipts sitting in suspense risk understating income. — [THP](https://www.thp.co.uk/what-is-a-suspense-account/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Client service firms list "unusual or one-off transactions" and "corrections to a period already filed for VAT" as must-ask items. — [1st Formations](https://help.1stformations.co.uk/accounting-service/when-to-contact-us-vs-when-to-handle-it-yourself)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** The practical rule set:
  - Code it yourself when the supplier or narrative makes the nature clear and no VAT or tax judgement depends on unknown facts.
  - Query when the answer changes VAT recovery (attendees), tax deductibility (client entertaining is disallowable for corporation tax and income tax), personal vs business use (DLA or benefit in kind), or capital vs revenue above the threshold. Also query any unidentified receipt.
  - For a meal with no attendee information, the cautious default is to treat it as entertainment and not reclaim VAT. A query is then worth raising only if the recoverable VAT is worth the client's time.

### Gaps
- No practitioner survey quantifies how often expense claims are queried, or a typical £ floor for sundry coding.

## 6. Review hierarchy and thresholds by client size/risk

### Takeaway
ICAEW requires risk-based review: senior reviews junior, principal reviews by risk, hot review before finalising, cold sample review, and external or reciprocal review for sole practitioners. Practitioner descriptions show a layered sign-off: preparer, then senior (technical accuracy), then manager (quality control), then partner or principal for higher-risk work. I found no published guidance on different numeric thresholds per level. Thresholds scale with client size and risk through judgement.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** ICAEW PA Standard 4 (smaller firms, 2018):
  - Review all work before it is finalised, except for sole practitioners with no staff.
  - Set review levels on a risk basis, with senior staff reviewing junior staff.
  - Cold-review a sample, focusing on higher-risk work, and make cold reviews of high-risk files essential.
  - Keep review evidence and document judgements.
  - — [ICAEW PA Standard 4](https://www.icaew.com/regulation/practice-assurance/practice-assurance-standards-smaller-firms/standard-4-smaller)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Described layered model: the prep team drafts, a senior checks technical accuracy, a manager does a quality-control review, then the pack is sent. — via search summary of [AccountingWEB: Do NOT review employees work](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/any-answers/do-not-review-employees-work) and related threads (attribution to a specific thread is uncertain)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Reviewer best practice: read exceptions first, trace a sample to source, and record reasons for approve, correct or return. — [AccountingWEB: How to review AI-prepared accounting work](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/community/industry-insights/how-to-review-ai-prepared-accounting-work)
- **[OFFICIAL]** AAT licensed members keep full responsibility for compliance, and supervision does not transfer it. — [AAT licensed member client monitoring checklist](https://www.aat.org.uk/files/AAT-licensed-member-client-monitoring-review-checklist.docx)

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** In practice:
  - **Bookkeeper:** codes routine items and parks unknowns for query or suspense.
  - **Senior:** clears or approves suspense and sundry, drafts the query list, posts year-end adjustments within the client's de minimis.
  - **Manager or partner:** sets each client's policies (capitalisation, accrual floors), approves judgement calls on material or sensitive items, and decides what blocks sign-off.
  - Clients that are higher-risk or larger get lower relative thresholds in practice: more review, lower tolerance for sundry coding, and more queries.

### Gaps
- No UK source publishes per-level £ thresholds or a standard review-point template for compilation work.
- ACCA and ICAS practice-review guidance equivalents were not retrieved.
